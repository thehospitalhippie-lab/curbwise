How to get rid of leaves, brush and yard waste in Nashville

Yard waste is banned from your trash cart. Metro picks up brush and paper-bagged leaves at the curb four times a year, and Davidson County residents can drop it off free in between.

Checked Nov 6, 2026 against official Nashville pages · Researched by Yeha, an AI assistant

Quick answer: Don’t put yard waste in your trash cart, because Metro bans it from the trash. If you live in a single-family home in Metro Nashville, Metro picks up brush and paper-bagged leaves and grass at the curb four times a year. Look up your route’s start date and set everything out before it. Can’t wait? Davidson County residents can drop yard waste off free at two Living Earth sites.

At the curb: Metro’s brush and leaf pickup

Brush pickup is run by the Nashville Department of Transportation (NDOT), not Waste Services. Crews collect brush and yard waste four times a year on a rotating schedule that covers 12 routes.

  • Who gets it: single-family homes in both the Urban Services District and the General Services District.
  • Who doesn’t: homes on private streets, and satellite cities such as Belle Meade, Berry Hill and Forest Hills. If you live in one of those cities, ask your city what it offers. If you rent an apartment or condo, ask your property manager.
  • Find your dates: use Metro’s Waste Wizard lookup (see NDOT’s brush page) or the Nashville Waste and Recycling app. Brush pickup shows as a yellow dot on your route’s start date. Because pickup is quarterly, you won’t see a date every month.
  • Be patient: crews usually take one to three weeks to finish a route, so your pile may not go on day one.

How to set it out

  • When: before your route’s start date.
  • Where: wherever your garbage is collected, at the curb or in the alley.
  • Leaves and grass clippings: in biodegradable paper bags only, not plastic.
  • Branches: up to 4 inches thick and 12 feet long.
  • Stumps: up to 8 inches across, with no dirt or root balls. NDOT’s length limit for stumps isn’t clear, so call 615-862-5000 to confirm.
  • Only yard waste: limbs, branches and true yard waste. Nothing else goes in the pile.
  • Don’t block fire hydrants, storm drains, ditches, culverts, alleys, sidewalks, bike lanes or travel lanes.

Can’t wait? Drop it off free

Two yard-waste sites managed by Living Earth take brush, leaves and other yard waste from Davidson County residents.

  • Cost: Free at Living Earth: 1511 Elm Hill Pike or 6401 Centennial Blvd (Davidson County residents; trailers are charged a fee; hours vary).
  • Hours: they change through the year. Call Living Earth at 615-730-7238 before you go.

Keep it: compost at home

If you’d rather compost your leaves and grass clippings, Metro sells backyard compost bins at the East Convenience Center for $50.

What not to do

  • Don’t use your trash cart or a dumpster. Metro bans brush, leaves and grass clippings from trash collection.
  • Don’t take it to a Convenience Center. Brush and leaves are on the centers’ not-accepted list.
  • Don’t burn it. Nashville’s Fire Marshal’s Office says you can’t get a permit to burn brush or leaves in your yard.

After a big storm

After Winter Storm Fern in early 2026, NDOT ran a special storm-debris collection for months before regular brush pickup came back in May. After a big storm, check NDOT’s latest notice before you pile up debris, because the rules can be different.

Questions about brush pickup: call 311 or 615-862-5000, or use hubNashville.

Rather not lift a thing?

Paid junk-removal services pick up from inside your home or the curb, and many donate usable items. It costs more than dropping it off yourself, but it's the fastest option.

  • LoadUp — Book online with an upfront price; pickup from inside your home or outside.
  • College HUNKS Hauling Junk — Nashville junk removal; book online or get a free estimate.
