Does Nashville pick up bulk trash? What Metro takes at the curb

Metro's curbside service takes bagged trash in your cart, recycling every other week, and brush four times a year. Couches, mattresses, extra bags and other big stuff go to a Convenience Center instead.

Checked Nov 7, 2026 against official Nashville pages · Researched by Yeha, an AI assistant

Quick answer: No. Metro doesn’t pick up couches, mattresses, appliances or other bulky items at the curb, and trash crews won’t take bags or boxes left beside your cart. Take them to a Convenience Center instead. There, 1 bulk item plus 3 bags of trash is free once a day, but mattresses cost $12 each. The one curbside exception is brush and leaves, which Metro picks up four times a year.

What Metro picks up at the curb

Metro’s curbside trash and recycling service is for single-family homes in the Urban Services District (USD). That includes duplexes, triplexes and quadplexes.

  • Trash: every week, bagged, inside your brown cart.
  • Recycling: every other week, loose (not bagged), inside your recycling cart.
  • Brush and leaves: four times a year. NDOT runs this pickup for single-family homes in both the USD and the General Services District, but not on private streets or in satellite cities. See our leaves, brush and yard waste guide.
  • Bulky items: no. Metro’s bulky item page says anything too big for your cart belongs at a Convenience Center.
  • Anything outside the cart: no. Extra bags, boxes and other items set next to your cart won’t be picked up.

Outside the USD, or in an apartment?

  • Outside the USD: Metro doesn’t collect household trash, so you’ll need to hire a private hauler. Metro requires private haulers to offer recycling to their trash customers, so ask for it. In a satellite city such as Belle Meade or Berry Hill? Ask your city what it offers.
  • Apartments and other multi-family buildings: ask your property manager about trash and recycling. Inside the USD, Metro provides dumpster service to a complex if the owner or manager asks for it.
  • Either way, you can still take household waste to the Convenience Centers. Bring a Tennessee driver’s license showing a Davidson County address.
  • Not sure which district you’re in? Call Waste Services at 615-862-5000 to confirm.

Too much for your cart? Three fixes

1. Drop the extra at a Convenience Center

Metro’s own advice is to take excess trash to a Convenience Center.

  • The free visit (3 bags of trash, 30 gallons max each, plus 1 bulk item): Free at All four centers (1 visit a day. Excludes mattresses, tires with rims, and construction debris).
  • More than that: under a cubic yard is $10 at All four centers (Up to 2 visits a day; load can't exceed the truck bed). Loads of 1 to 8 cubic yards are $20 per cubic yard at All four centers (1 visit a day. No construction debris; no trailers over 12 ft; no cargo vans or trailers at Anderson Lane).
  • Where: any of the four centers: Anderson Lane Convenience Center — 939A Anderson Ln, Madison, TN 37115East Convenience Center — 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207Ezell Pike Convenience Center — 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211Omohundro Convenience Center — 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210
  • Hours: Tuesday–Saturday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Closed on Metro holidays (dates below).
  • Bring: a Tennessee driver’s license with a Davidson County address. Bag your household trash, and plan to unload it yourself. Household waste only: no commercial waste, and no vehicles with commercial advertising or commercial tags.
  • Paying: cash (not at Omohundro), check or card. Cards add a 2.8% fee, minimum $1.95.

2. Get a second trash cart

If your cart is full every week, you can get one extra trash cart for $65. Ask for it through hubNashville online or by calling 311, or call Waste Services at 615-862-5000.

3. Recycle more of it

Recycling carts are free, and you can have up to two. Request one with hubNashville’s cart service form. The extra room helps with cardboard, which isn’t allowed in your trash cart. Put boxes in your recycling cart, or take them to a Convenience Center or one of Metro’s recycling drop-off sites.

Where big items go instead

  • Couches, chairs, dressers and tables: one piece is free with your daily free visit. See couches and furniture.
  • Mattresses and box springs: $12 each at East, Ezell Pike, Omohundro (4 a month). See mattresses.
  • Fridges, washers, stoves and other appliances: Metro recycles them as scrap metal, and one item (or up to three bags of scrap metal) is free. See appliances, and read it first for fridges and freezers.
  • TVs and electronics: Free at East, Ezell Pike, Omohundro (Up to 100 lbs a day, including 1 TV no wider than 80 inches; 1 computer system a month). See TVs and electronics.
  • Tires: without rims, Free at East, Ezell Pike (4 tires a month). On rims, $5 per tire at East, Ezell Pike (4 tires a month). See tires.
  • Oil-based paint and household chemicals: Free at East, Ezell Pike (Up to 15 gallons or 100 lbs per household a month; 1 visit a day). See paint and chemicals.
  • Remodel debris: small do-it-yourself loads are $20 at Omohundro only (Up to 2 cubic yards on Metro's fee table, but its bulky item page says 1, so call to confirm. 1 visit a day). For bigger jobs, Metro says to use a private hauler or your contractor.

What can’t go in your trash cart

Even if it fits, Metro bans these from curbside trash:

  • Yard waste (brush, leaves and grass clippings): save it for your next brush pickup, or drop it off free. See yard waste.
  • Cardboard: use your recycling cart or a recycling drop-off site.
  • Electronics: recycle them free at three Convenience Centers (see above).
  • Construction debris: hire a private hauler or have your contractor take it. Small DIY loads can go to Omohundro.
  • Liquids, paint and chemicals: no liquids in the trash. Let latex paint dry out before you toss the can. Oil-based paint and chemicals go to the free hazardous-waste drop-off.

Cart rules, so you don’t get skipped

  • Set it out by 6 a.m. on pickup day, at the curb or in the alley, with the arrow on the lid pointing toward the street or alley. Crews come between 6 a.m. and 5 p.m.
  • Give it room. Keep it 4 feet from cars, mailboxes, poles and your other cart, with 15 feet of clear space overhead (no wires, branches or overhangs).
  • Bring it in by 7 p.m. After pickup, Metro code says to store carts somewhere secure and out of view of the street.
  • Cart lost, stolen or damaged beyond repair? A replacement costs $65, and the owner pays.
  • Missed pickup? Report it to hubNashville online or by calling 311, or call Waste Services at 615-862-5000.

Find your pickup day

Pickup days changed the week of February 2, 2026, when Metro moved trash and recycling to a Tuesday–Friday schedule. Find your day in the Nashville Waste and Recycling app for iPhone or Android, which can also send you reminders. NDOT posts brush pickup dates there too. Download links are on Metro’s Collection Calendar and Waste Wizard page.

Holiday weeks through New Year’s

Metro updated its holiday schedule for the rest of 2026. When a holiday lands on your pickup day, your cart is picked up on a Monday instead:

  • Veterans Day, Wednesday, Nov 11: Wednesday routes are picked up Monday, Nov 9. That’s two days early.
  • Thanksgiving, Thursday, Nov 26: Thursday routes are picked up Monday, Nov 30. Friday, Nov 27 is a normal pickup day.
  • Christmas, Friday, Dec 25: Friday routes are picked up Monday, Dec 28. Thursday, Dec 24 is a normal pickup day.
  • New Year’s Day, Friday, Jan 1, 2027: Friday routes are picked up Monday, Jan 4.

Convenience Centers close on Metro holidays. Through New Year’s, they’re closed Nov 11, Nov 26 and 27, Dec 24 and 25, and Jan 1.

Questions: call Waste Services at 615-862-5000, or contact hubNashville online or at 311.
