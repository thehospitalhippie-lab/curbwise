How to get rid of paint and household chemicals in Nashville

It depends what's in the can. Latex paint can be dried out and trashed. Oil-based paint and household chemicals go to Metro's free hazardous-waste drop-off.

Checked Nov 9, 2026 against official Nashville pages · Researched by Yeha, an AI assistant

Quick answer: Latex (water-based) paint isn’t hazardous waste, but it can’t go to the landfill wet. Use it up or give it away, or let it dry out completely and then bag the can for your regular trash. Oil-based paint and household chemicals go free to Metro’s drop-off at the East or Ezell Pike Convenience Center (up to 15 gallons or 100 lbs per household a month; 1 visit a day).

Step 1: Figure out what kind of paint you have

Check the label. Latex paint says “latex,” “water-based” or “acrylic.” Oil-based paint says “oil-based” or “alkyd,” and words like “mineral spirits,” “turpentine” or “flammable” also point to oil-based. Can’t tell? Play it safe and treat it as oil-based.

Latex paint: use it, share it, or dry it out

Latex paint is low in toxicity and isn’t hazardous waste. Metro’s hazardous-waste drop-off stopped taking it in 2014 and still doesn’t. But don’t toss a can of wet paint: in Tennessee, it’s illegal to send wet latex paint to a landfill. Instead:

  1. Use it up or give it away. Keep some for touch-ups, or offer it to a neighbor or a group that can use it. If you store it, Metro suggests stretching plastic wrap over the opening before you put the lid back on.
  2. An inch of paint or less in the can? Take the lid off and let it dry in a spot with good airflow, away from kids and pets.
  3. More than an inch? Add kitty litter, sawdust, shredded paper or mulch, or a paint hardener from a store that sells paint, to dry it out.
  4. Once it’s completely dry, put the can in a trash bag and into your regular trash.

Never pour paint down a sink, toilet or storm drain. In Tennessee, dumping paint down a sink or into a storm drain is illegal, and latex paint can clog pipes and cause sewer overflows.

Oil-based paint and household chemicals: free drop-off

Oil-based paint is flammable, so it counts as household hazardous waste and can’t go in the trash. Use it up or give it away if you can. Otherwise, take it and your other household chemicals to Metro’s drop-off:

  • Free, up to 15 gallons or 100 lbs per household a month; 1 visit a day.
  • Where: East Convenience Center — 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207Ezell Pike Convenience Center — 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211
  • Hours: Tuesday–Saturday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Closed on Metro holidays.
  • Bring a Tennessee driver’s license that shows a Davidson County address. It’s for Davidson County residents only.
  • Household waste only: no hazardous waste from a business, and nothing in a commercial or work vehicle. That includes any vehicle with commercial advertising or commercial tags.

What the drop-off takes

Metro’s list includes:

  • Paint and finishes: oil-based paint, stain (not water-based), varnish, paint thinner, caulk and adhesives
  • Car and garage: motor oil, antifreeze, brake fluid, transmission fluid, gasoline, windshield wiper fluid, car wax and cleaners, and lighter fluid
  • Cleaners: bleach, household cleaners, drain opener, oven cleaner, toilet bowl cleaner, tub and tile cleaner, spot remover, detergent and shoe polish
  • Bugs, weeds and pests: pesticides, insecticide, weed killer, rat and mouse poison, insect repellent, flea collars, pet spray and dip, and mothballs
  • Pool: pool chemicals
  • Batteries, bulbs and mercury: rechargeable batteries, car batteries and other lead-acid batteries, fluorescent tubes, CFL bulbs and mercury thermometers
  • Kitchen: cooking grease (see cooking oil and grease)

Pack it so nothing spills

These are Metro’s tips for getting it there safely:

  • Keep everything in its original container if you can. If you can’t, use a plastic jug with a lid that closes tight, and write what’s inside on it.
  • Don’t mix liquids together.
  • Pack the containers in a sturdy box or a flat-bottomed tote lined with newspaper, so a leak stays put in your trunk or truck bed.
  • Keep it all away from kids and pets.

What the drop-off won’t take

  • Latex or acrylic paint: dry it out, as described above.
  • Unused or expired medicine: Metro Nashville Police takes it at 10 locations (its precincts and headquarters), weekdays 8 a.m.–4 p.m. It takes prescription, over-the-counter and pet medicines, inhalers, and liquids in leak-proof containers. It doesn’t take needles, thermometers, blood sugar equipment or IV bags. (Mercury thermometers can go to the hazardous-waste drop-off.) Addresses are on the police department’s page (see Sources).
  • Needles: Metro says you can put them in a red sharps container, or a 2-liter bottle with the lid on, and put that in your trash. Other medical waste isn’t accepted at the drop-off.
  • Water-based stains, propane tanks, ammunition and explosives: not accepted, and Metro’s pages don’t say where they go. Fireworks and flares aren’t accepted at any Convenience Center, either.
  • More than the monthly limit, or waste from outside Davidson County: not accepted.

Questions about household hazardous waste, including the items above: call Metro at 615-862-5000.
