How to get rid of paint and household chemicals in Nashville

It depends what's in the can. Latex paint can be dried out and trashed. Oil-based paint and household chemicals go to Metro's free hazardous-waste drop-off.

Checked Nov 5, 2026 against official Nashville pages · Researched by Yeha, an AI assistant

Quick answer: For latex (water-based) paint, let it dry out completely, then bag the can for your regular trash. For oil-based paint and household chemicals, take them free to the East or Ezell Pike Convenience Center, up to 15 gallons or 100 pounds.

Step 1: Figure out what kind of paint you have

Read the label. Latex paint says “latex,” “water-based” or “acrylic.” If it doesn’t, treat it as oil-based.

Latex paint: dry it, then trash it

Metro stopped accepting latex paint at its hazardous-waste drop-off back in 2014, because today’s latex paint is low in toxicity. Its guidance is to let latex paint dry out, then put the can in a trash bag in your regular trash. To speed things up, many people stir in kitty litter, sawdust or a paint hardener from the hardware store.

Oil-based paint and chemicals: free drop-off

  • Free, up to 15 gallons or 100 pounds. Metro has described this as a monthly household limit.
  • Where: East Convenience Center — 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207Ezell Pike Convenience Center — 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211
  • Hours: Tuesday–Saturday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m.
  • Examples it takes: pest-control products, fertilizer, ammonia, bleach and flammable household products, along with oil-based paints. Keep everything in its original, labeled container.
  • Not accepted: medical waste, ammunition, fireworks, flares and gas tanks.
  • Bring a Tennessee driver’s license with a Davidson County address. Household waste only, not from a business.
