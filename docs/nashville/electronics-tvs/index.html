How to get rid of a TV, computer or other electronics in Nashville

Electronics are banned from Nashville household trash, but recycling them is free at three Convenience Centers.

Checked Nov 8, 2026 against official Nashville pages · Researched by Yeha, an AI assistant

Quick answer: Recycling electronics is free at the East, Ezell Pike and Omohundro Convenience Centers, up to 100 pounds a day. You can bring 1 TV a day, up to 80 inches wide. Don’t put electronics in your trash cart, because Metro bans them from household trash.

What Metro takes

Phones and smartphones, computers and computer parts (cables, keyboards, mice, hubs, external drives), monitors, printers, copiers, scanners, fax machines, TVs, microwaves, coffee makers and other kitchen appliances, hair dryers, electric toothbrushes and other bathroom appliances, stereos, radios, cassette and CD players, DVD and VCR players, tablets, e-readers, MP3 players, pagers and other handheld electronics, string lights, rechargeable batteries, and music or video cassettes and discs.

Big appliances like fridges, stoves and washers are recycled as scrap metal instead. See appliances.

The rules

  • Free, with no fee at all.
  • Up to 100 lbs a day, including 1 TV a day. The TV can’t be wider than 80 inches.
  • One a day of the bigger items: a computer system (computer and monitor), a microwave, coffee maker or other kitchen appliance, a desktop copier, printer or scanner, a fax machine, a radio, cassette player or CD player, a DVD or VCR player, and a stereo (not a cabinet stereo).
  • Computers: Metro also limits you to one computer system a month.
  • Household electronics only. No business equipment, no truck or trailer loads, nothing in a work or commercial vehicle, and no out-of-county residents.
  • Bring a Tennessee driver’s license showing a Davidson County address.

Where: East Convenience Center — 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207Ezell Pike Convenience Center — 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211Omohundro Convenience Center — 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210

Hours are Tuesday–Saturday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m., and the centers close on Metro holidays. (Anderson Lane doesn’t take electronics.)

Before you drop it off

Wipe your phones, tablets and computers. Sign out of your accounts, do a factory reset, and take out any memory cards or discs you want to keep.

Rather not lift a thing?

Paid junk-removal services pick up from inside your home or the curb, and many donate usable items. It costs more than dropping it off yourself, but it's the fastest option.
