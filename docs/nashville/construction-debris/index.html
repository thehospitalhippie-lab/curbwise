How to get rid of construction debris and remodel waste in Nashville

Construction debris can't go in your trash cart, and Metro won't haul it away. One Convenience Center takes small do-it-yourself loads. For anything bigger, Metro says to use a private hauler or your contractor.

Checked Nov 10, 2026 against official Nashville pages · Researched by Yeha, an AI assistant

Quick answer: Small do-it-yourself loads go to the Omohundro Convenience Center, the only Metro center that takes construction and demolition debris. It costs $20 a load, 1 visit a day, and Omohundro doesn’t take cash. Keep the load to 1 cubic yard unless Metro tells you 2 is OK, because its pages disagree. Brick, concrete, rock, dirt, roofing shingles, siding and asbestos aren’t accepted at Metro’s Convenience Centers. For bigger jobs, Metro says to hire a private hauler or have your contractor take it.

Small do-it-yourself loads: Omohundro only

Of Metro’s four Convenience Centers, only Omohundro takes construction and demolition material. It’s the place for debris from a small project you did yourself.

  • Cost: $20 a load. Construction debris is never part of the free daily load (3 bags of trash and 1 bulk item).
  • How much: Metro’s fee table says up to 2 cubic yards, but its bulky-item page says 1. Call 615-862-5000 to confirm before you bring more than 1 cubic yard. (One cubic yard is a pile 3 feet long, 3 feet wide and 3 feet high.)
  • How often: 1 visit a day.
  • Where: Omohundro Convenience Center — 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210
  • Hours: Tuesday–Saturday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Closed on Metro holidays (the dates are in our curbside pickup guide).
  • Pay by check or card. Omohundro doesn’t take cash. Cards carry a 2.8% fee, with a $1.95 minimum.
  • Bring a Tennessee driver’s license that shows a Davidson County address.
  • You unload your own load.
  • Household loads only: no commercial waste, and nothing in a commercial vehicle. That includes any vehicle with commercial advertising or commercial tags.

What the Convenience Centers won’t take

These common remodeling leftovers are on Metro’s Convenience Center not-accepted list:

  • Asbestos, such as asbestos shingles or flooring
  • Brick, concrete, masonry, rock and dirt
  • Roofing shingles, siding, gutters and plumbing
  • Chain-link and rebar
  • Pallets, poles, posts and railroad ties

Metro’s fee table mentions a list of accepted construction materials, but the list isn’t posted. Not sure about your load, like drywall, lumber, old cabinets or flooring? Call 615-862-5000 before you go.

Asbestos: Metro’s pages don’t say where it goes. Call 615-862-5000 to ask before you tear out anything that might contain it.

Still usable? Donate it first

Metro’s advice to builders is to send material to a landfill only when there’s no better use for it. Cabinets, doors and leftover lumber often have one.

Habitat for Humanity ReStore, 414 Harding Place, Nashville. Call 615-942-1291. ReStores take donations of new and gently used building materials, appliances and furniture. Habitat says most ReStores accept kitchen cabinets, doors, windows, flooring, unused lumber, light fixtures, fencing, and bricks or blocks. Each store sets its own list, so call to check your items and the donation hours, which can differ from store hours. Many ReStores pick up large items, so ask whether this one can.

Sort it to recycle it

  • Carpet and padding: Metro’s 2026 recycling list says the East, Ezell Pike and Omohundro Convenience Centers take them from Davidson County residents. Carpet isn’t on Metro’s fee table, and items that aren’t listed are charged by the size of the load, so call 615-862-5000 to check the cost.
  • Concrete, brick, metal, wood, shingles and more: Metro’s bulky-item page links a list of companies that recycle construction materials like these. The list dates from 2023, and Metro notes that some of these companies may only take commercial loads. Call first to check fees and rules.
  • Paint, stain, caulk and adhesives: oil-based paint and stain, varnish, paint thinner, caulk and adhesives go free to Metro’s hazardous-waste drop-off at East or Ezell Pike (up to 15 gallons or 100 lbs per household a month; 1 visit a day). Fluorescent tubes go there too. Latex paint and water-based stain don’t: dry them out with the lid off or with cat litter, then trash the can. See paint and chemicals.
  • Old appliances: see appliances.
  • Brush, stumps and trees from clearing a lot: see leaves, brush and yard waste.

Bigger jobs: a contractor, a hauler or a dumpster

Metro’s rule is that residential construction waste must be collected by a private hauler or a contractor.

  • Hiring a contractor? Ask whether hauling away the debris is part of the job.
  • Hiring a hauler or renting a dumpster? Metro requires anyone who collects or hauls waste in Davidson County to get a permit every year for each vehicle and each roll-off container. Ask the company whether it has one. Permit questions: 615-767-2631.
  • Hauling a big load yourself? Metro’s bulky-item page lists a few private sites that take larger loads and says to contact each one about fees. We couldn’t confirm which of them take loads from homeowners, so call before you go.

What not to do

  • Don’t put it in your trash cart. Metro bans construction debris from curbside trash.
  • Don’t leave it at the curb. Metro doesn’t pick up items left outside your cart. See our curbside pickup guide.
  • Don’t dump it on an empty lot, along a road or in a dumpster that isn’t yours.

Not sure where something goes? Metro’s Waste Wizard, in the Nashville Waste and Recycling app, looks up where to take materials, or you can call 615-924-2633. Questions about Omohundro: call Waste Services at 615-862-5000.

Hiring a junk hauler instead? Ask whether they take construction debris before you book.

Rather not lift a thing?

Paid junk-removal services pick up from inside your home or the curb, and many donate usable items. It costs more than dropping it off yourself, but it's the fastest option.

  • LoadUp — Book online with an upfront price; pickup from inside your home or outside.
  • College HUNKS Hauling Junk — Nashville junk removal; book online or get a free estimate.
