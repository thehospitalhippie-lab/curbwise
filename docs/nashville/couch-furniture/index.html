How to get rid of a couch or furniture in Nashville

One piece of furniture per visit is free at any of Metro's four Convenience Centers. Usable furniture can go to a ReStore instead.

Checked Nov 5, 2026 against official Nashville pages · Researched by Yeha, an AI assistant

Quick answer: One bulk item, like a couch, dresser or table, is free at any of the four Convenience Centers, along with up to 3 bags of trash. More than that is charged by volume: $10 for under a cubic yard, or $20 per cubic yard for bigger loads.

Free: one piece per visit at a Convenience Center

Metro lists couches, chairs, sofas, dressers, mirrors, tables, toilets, vehicle seats and windows as bulk items it takes for disposal.

  • The free visit: Free at All four centers (1 visit a day. Excludes mattresses, tires with rims, and construction debris.).
  • Extra pieces: less than a cubic yard is $10 at All four centers (Up to 2 visits a day; load can't exceed the truck bed). Bigger loads are $20 per cubic yard at All four centers (No construction debris; no trailers over 12 ft; no cargo vans or trailers at Anderson Lane).
  • Where: any of the four centers: Anderson Lane Convenience Center — 939A Anderson Ln, Madison, TN 37115East Convenience Center — 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207Ezell Pike Convenience Center — 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211Omohundro Convenience Center — 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210
  • Hours: Tuesday–Saturday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Closed on Metro holidays.
  • Bring: a Tennessee driver’s license with a Davidson County address. You unload your own load, and it must be household furniture, not from a business.

Metro’s own advice is to donate furniture that still has life in it before you throw it out.

Donate it

Habitat for Humanity ReStore, 414 Harding Place, Nashville. Call 615-942-1291. ReStores resell donated furniture and appliances to fund Habitat’s homebuilding, and most offer pickup for large donations. Call or check their site to see what they’ll take and whether pickup is available in your area.

Don’t leave it at the curb

Metro’s bulky item page says anything too big for your curbside cart should go to a Convenience Center.

Rather not lift a thing?

Paid junk-removal services pick up from inside your home or the curb, and many donate usable items. It costs more than dropping it off yourself, but it's the fastest option.

  • LoadUp — Book online with an upfront price; pickup from inside your home or outside.
  • College HUNKS Hauling Junk — Nashville junk removal; book online or get a free estimate.
