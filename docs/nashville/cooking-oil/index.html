How to get rid of cooking oil and grease in Nashville

Grease poured down a drain can clog your pipes and cause sewer overflows in your neighborhood. Here's what Metro says to do with it instead, from a pan of bacon grease to a turkey fryer full of oil.

Checked Nov 9, 2026 against official Nashville pages · Researched by Yeha, an AI assistant

Quick answer: Never pour cooking oil or grease down a sink, toilet or other drain. For small amounts, let it cool in a small container, close the lid tight, bag it and put it in your trash. For a turkey fryer or deep fryer full of oil, take it to the East or Ezell Pike Convenience Center, where dropping off household cooking grease is free. Omohundro takes it too. The centers are closed for Thanksgiving on Nov 26 and 27.

Small amounts: seal it and trash it

This is Metro Water Services’ method for everyday grease, like bacon fat or a little leftover frying oil:

  1. Pour it into a small container with a lid and let it cool. (Grease like bacon fat will harden.)
  2. When the container is full, close the lid tight or cover it well. Put it in a bag so it can’t leak, then put it in your trash.
  3. Before you wash greasy pots, pans and plates, wipe them out with a paper towel. Throw the towel in the trash.
  4. Go easy on your garbage disposal. Grease and food bits that go through it can still clog your pipes and the sewer.

A fryer full of oil: drop it off

A turkey fryer or deep fryer leaves you with far more oil than a small container can hold. Metro takes household cooking oil and grease at three Convenience Centers: East, Ezell Pike and Omohundro.

  • East and Ezell Pike: free. Cooking grease is one of the items Metro’s household hazardous waste drop-off takes at these two centers: East Convenience Center — 943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207Ezell Pike Convenience Center — 3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211
  • Omohundro also takes it. Metro’s hazardous-waste page says Omohundro accepts cooking grease too, but Metro doesn’t post a fee for it there. Call 615-862-5000 if you want to check first: Omohundro Convenience Center — 1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210
  • Hours: Tuesday–Saturday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Closed on Metro holidays.
  • Bring: a Tennessee driver’s license showing a Davidson County address. Cooking-oil drop-off is for Davidson County residents only.
  • Household oil only: no oil from a restaurant or other business, and nothing in a work or commercial vehicle. That includes any vehicle with commercial advertising or commercial tags.
  • How much: Metro’s hazardous-waste drop-off, which lists cooking grease, takes up to 15 gallons or 100 lbs per household a month; 1 visit a day.

Getting it there without a spill

First, let the oil cool completely before you pour it or move it. Then follow Metro’s tips for hauling household hazardous waste, which work for cooking oil too:

  • Keep it in its original jug if you can. If not, use a plastic jug with a lid that closes tight, and label it “cooking oil.”
  • Don’t mix anything else into it.
  • Pack the jugs in a sturdy box or a flat-bottomed tote, lined with newspaper, so a spill stays put. Keep them away from kids and pets.

Frying a turkey for Thanksgiving?

Convenience Centers close on Metro holidays, so they’re closed Thursday, Nov 26 and Friday, Nov 27. Seal the cooled oil in jugs and store it somewhere safe until they reopen on Saturday, Nov 28. (The centers are always closed on Sundays and Mondays.)

Trashing a small container of grease instead? Thanksgiving week, Thursday trash routes are picked up the following Monday, Nov 30, and Friday routes run as usual. More holiday dates are in our curbside pickup guide.

What not to do

  • Don’t pour it down any drain. That means no kitchen sinks, bathroom sinks or toilets. Grease clogs your home’s pipes and Metro’s sewer lines, and those clogs can cause sewer overflows in your neighborhood. Overflows pollute Nashville’s streams and are a health hazard for people, pets and wildlife.
  • Don’t pour it loose into your trash cart. Seal it in a container and bag it first.
  • Don’t dump it outside or into a storm drain. Take it to the East, Ezell Pike or Omohundro Convenience Center instead.

Questions about grease and sewer clogs: call Metro Water Services at 615-862-4600. Questions about drop-off: call Waste Services at 615-862-5000.

Hiring a junk hauler instead? Ask whether they take cooking oil before you book.
