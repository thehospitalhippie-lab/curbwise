**Quick answer:** Take it to any Convenience Center. Metro recycles appliances as **scrap metal**, and **one bulk item (or up to three bags) is free**. Working appliances can go to a ReStore.
</div>

## Free: recycle it as scrap metal

Metro's examples include refrigerators, ovens and ranges, washers and dryers, dishwashers, barbecue grills and other large household appliances.

- **Cost:** one bulk item or up to three bags of scrap metal free. Extra loads are charged by volume ({{ fee_text('volume_small') }}).
- **Where:** any of the four centers: {{ centers() }}
- **Hours:** Tuesday–Saturday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m.
- **Bring:** a Tennessee driver's license with a Davidson County address, plus help lifting. You unload it yourself.

**Fridges, freezers and window AC units** hold refrigerant. Metro's pages don't spell out a refrigerant rule, so call 615-862-5000 before you haul one over to avoid a wasted trip.

## Still works? Donate it

Habitat for Humanity's **ReStore** (414 Harding Place, 615-942-1291) takes donated appliances to resell, and most ReStores pick up large items. Call first, because each store sets its own rules on age and condition.

## Buying a new one?

Ask the store whether delivery includes haul-away of the old one. It often costs less than hiring someone separately.
