**Quick answer:** **Alkaline batteries** (the label says "alkaline") can go in your trash. **Lithium, rechargeable and car batteries** can't go in your trash or recycling cart. Take lithium-ion, rechargeable and car batteries to the **East or Ezell Pike Convenience Center**, where they're **{{ fee('batteries')['fee'] | lower }}** to drop off. **CFL bulbs and fluorescent tubes** go free to the same two centers. **Propane tanks** aren't taken at Metro's hazardous-waste drop-off, so call Metro at 615-862-5000 to ask where to take one.
</div>

## First, check the label

- **Alkaline:** the label says "alkaline."
- **Lithium or lithium-ion:** the label says "lithium" or "Li-ion." These run phones, laptops, power tools, e-bikes, power banks and earbuds, and they also turn up in talking greeting cards and disposable vapes.
- **Other rechargeables:** the label says "rechargeable," "NiMH" or "NiCd."
- **Car batteries:** car, truck and other lead-acid batteries.

Can't tell what you have? Keep it out of the trash and call Metro at 615-862-5000 before you bring it.

## Alkaline batteries: the trash is fine

Tennessee's environment department (TDEC) says alkaline batteries sold after May 1996 have no added mercury, so you can put them in your regular trash. They don't need to be recycled, and Metro's hazardous-waste drop-off doesn't list them.

## Lithium-ion and rechargeable batteries: free drop-off, never the cart

**Keep them out of your trash cart, your recycling cart and any dumpster.** Metro and the Nashville Fire Marshal warn that lithium-ion batteries can catch fire if they're crushed, punctured or overheated, and that they're a serious fire risk in curbside carts. TDEC adds that lithium batteries thrown in the trash or recycling can get damaged in trucks and compactors and start fires. TDEC also says NiMH and NiCd rechargeables must be recycled, not trashed.

**Drop them off free.** Rechargeable batteries are on the list for Metro's household hazardous waste drop-off at East and Ezell Pike ({{ fee('hhw')['limits'] | lower }}). Metro's October 2026 fire-safety notice says these are the only two Convenience Centers that take lithium-ion batteries.

- **Where:** {{ centers('east', 'ezell') }}
- **Cost:** {{ fee('batteries')['fee'] }} for Davidson County residents. What to bring is below.

**Get them ready:**

1. **Tape the ends.** TDEC says to cover each battery's terminals (the metal ends or contacts) with electrical, packing or duct tape. Don't wrap the whole battery, so the type still shows.
2. **Keep them in a sturdy plastic container** until you drop them off, as TDEC suggests.
3. **Pack the container in a sturdy box or tote** lined with newspaper, the way Metro asks you to bring hazardous waste, and keep it away from kids and pets.

**Batteries still in a phone or laptop?** Phones, laptops, tablets and other small electronics go to Metro's free electronics drop-off at East, Ezell Pike or Omohundro. See [TVs and electronics]({{ url('/nashville/electronics-tvs/') }}). Metro's electronics page also lists rechargeable batteries, but its newer fire-safety notice names only East and Ezell Pike for lithium-ion batteries, so take loose rechargeable batteries to one of those two.

**Disposable vapes** have a lithium battery inside. TDEC warns that lithium batteries in trash and recycling bins often get damaged by compactors and start fires. Metro's drop-off pages don't mention vapes, so call 615-862-5000 before you bring one.

**Single-use lithium and button batteries** (like the coin-size batteries in key fobs and watches): Metro's pages name lithium-ion and rechargeable batteries, but they don't say whether the drop-off takes single-use lithium or button batteries. Call 615-862-5000 before you bring them. TDEC says lithium batteries must not go in the trash or recycling, so keep single-use lithium batteries out of your carts too.

### Hot, swollen, hissing or smoking?

Stop using it. Metro and the Fire Marshal say to stop using any battery that gets hot, swells, smokes or gives off a smell. For a lithium-ion battery that's overheating, hissing or bulging, TDEC says to:

- move it outdoors, away from anything that can burn and onto a surface that won't burn, if you can do that safely;
- leave it outside for at least 6 hours (if it's safe to move, a metal container with completely dry sand can hold it);
- call the fire department right away and ask what to do next.

Metro's notice doesn't say whether East or Ezell Pike take damaged batteries, so call 615-862-5000 to ask where one should go. (The Home Depot's store bins are only for batteries that aren't leaking.)

## Car batteries: free drop-off or an auto parts store

TDEC says car, truck and other lead-acid batteries can't go in the trash. You have two options:

- **Metro:** {{ fee('batteries')['fee'] }} at the East or Ezell Pike Convenience Center, as part of the hazardous-waste drop-off. The same ID and household rules apply (see below).
- **Auto parts stores:** TDEC says almost all auto parts stores take lead-acid batteries for recycling. Call ahead. Buying a new battery? Ask the store to take your old one.

## Store drop-off for rechargeable batteries

TDEC says rechargeable batteries can be recycled through Call2Recycle, and that several home improvement stores take power-tool batteries. For example:

- **The Home Depot** says its stores have free Call2Recycle bins for rechargeable batteries up to 11 pounds and under 300 watt-hours each. It says the bins don't take alkaline or other single-use batteries, and they're only for batteries that aren't leaking. Put your batteries in the plastic bags provided at the bin.

Store programs change, so call your store before you go.

## Light bulbs

**CFLs and fluorescent tubes** contain mercury. Metro takes them **free** at its hazardous-waste drop-off at East and Ezell Pike, and the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) recommends recycling them instead of putting them in the trash.

- **Pack them in a sturdy box** so they don't break on the way.
- **Mercury thermometers** go free to the same drop-off.
- **The Home Depot** says the recycling station at its store entrances takes CFL bulbs. Call ahead.
- **LED and regular (incandescent) bulbs:** Metro's drop-off lists only CFLs and fluorescent tubes, and the Metro pages we checked don't say where LED or incandescent bulbs go. Look them up in the Waste Wizard in Metro's Nashville Waste and Recycling app, or call 615-862-5000.

### Broke a CFL?

These are the EPA's cleanup steps, and the Tennessee Department of Health gives the same basic advice (air the room out, and don't vacuum):

1. Have people and pets leave the room. Open a window or an outside door for 5 to 10 minutes, and turn off central heat or air if you have it.
2. Don't vacuum. Scoop up the glass and powder with stiff paper or cardboard, pick up the small pieces with sticky tape, and wipe the spot with a damp paper towel.
3. Put everything in a glass jar with a metal lid or a plastic bag that seals, and take it outside right away.
4. Keep the room aired out, with the heat or air off, for several hours. Wash your hands.

The Tennessee Department of Health says broken mercury items can go to household hazardous waste collection, so take the sealed jar or bag to the drop-off at East or Ezell Pike.

## Propane tanks

Metro's hazardous-waste drop-off doesn't take propane tanks: they're on its not-accepted list. "Gas tanks" are also on the not-accepted list at all four Convenience Centers.

TDEC says propane cylinders can be recycled locally through your solid waste department. In Nashville, that's Metro Waste Services, but its drop-off doesn't take them. Call 615-862-5000 and ask where to take one before you load it up. Buying a new tank or a refill? Ask the seller whether it takes old tanks.

## What to bring to East or Ezell Pike

- **Free**, {{ fee('hhw')['limits'] | lower }}.
- **Where:** {{ centers('east', 'ezell') }}
- **Hours:** Tuesday–Saturday, 8:30 a.m.–4:30 p.m. Closed on Metro holidays (the dates are in our [curbside pickup guide]({{ url('/nashville/bulk-trash/') }})).
- **Bring** a Tennessee driver's license that shows a Davidson County address. It's for Davidson County residents only.
- **Household items only:** no batteries or bulbs from a business, and nothing in a commercial or work vehicle. That includes any vehicle with commercial advertising or commercial tags.

Questions about batteries, bulbs or propane: call Metro Waste Services at 615-862-5000. Oil-based paint and other household chemicals go to the same drop-off. See [paint and chemicals]({{ url('/nashville/paint-chemicals/') }}).

Hiring a junk hauler instead? Ask whether they take batteries, bulbs or propane tanks before you book.
